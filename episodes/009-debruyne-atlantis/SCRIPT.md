# 009 · Could prime Kevin De Bruyne find Atlantis with a pass?

Status: 📝 script, waiting for the creator's OK.
Estimated length: ~80 s. Sparse sound effects (only kick, splash, bubbles/pressure creak, stamp). No meter.

## 1 · Hook (~7 s)
> Could prime Kevin De Bruyne find Atlantis with a pass? We checked the map. … Plato's map.

**Animation:** De Bruyne stands on the cliff at Gibraltar with a ball at his feet, the open Atlantic ahead of him. A yellowed, ancient map unrolls on screen, with a big question mark in the middle of the ocean.

## 2 · The passer (~12 s)
> The passer: Kevin De Bruyne. In 2019–20, twenty Premier League assists. A joint record. … He finds team-mates nobody else can see.

**Animation:**
- An assist counter runs from 0 to 20 while passes fly into the picture from off-screen.
- A **JOINT RECORD (with Henry)** card appears.
- On "nobody else can see": De Bruyne plays a no-look pass behind his back, and a team-mate catches it far away in thick fog.

## 3 · The target (~12 s)
> The target: Atlantis. First described by Plato, around 360 BC. Somewhere beyond the Strait of Gibraltar. … It sank in a single day and night.

**Animation:**
- A golden city with temple and harbour rises in the middle of the sea, and a little Plato with a beard and toga points at it.
- A day/night clock spins once, and the city sinks slowly with bubbles.
- Card: **SINKING TIME: 1 day + 1 night**.

## 4 · Problem one: distance (~14 s)
> Problem one: distance. The Azores, a popular Atlantis candidate, are about one thousand eight hundred kilometres away. A long De Bruyne pass travels around sixty metres. … He needs thirty thousand passes. … And thirty thousand team-mates.

**Animation:**
- The camera zooms out to a map from Gibraltar to the Azores with a dashed line.
- Cards: **1,800 km** and **60 m**, then a counter to **30,000 passes**.
- On "team-mates" a chain of team-mates appears across the ocean, standing on buoys and rubber dinghies, and a pass is relayed from one to the next.

## 5 · Problem two: the water (~14 s)
> Problem two: the water. A football weighs four hundred and thirty grams, but displaces five and a half litres. It floats. … So he lets some air out. At the bottom of the Atlantic, the pressure is three hundred and sixty times higher. … The ball arrives completely flat.

**Animation:**
- The pass lands in the water, bobs up and down and stays on the surface. Card: **430 g vs 5.5 L → floats**.
- De Bruyne pulls out the valve (hiss) and the ball sinks.
- Cross-section view: a depth ruler runs down to **3,600 m**, and a pressure gauge on the ruler rattles up to **×360**.
- The ball is squeezed flatter and flatter on the way down until it lands on the seabed like a pancake.

## 6 · The end (~10 s)
> Atlantis? Still missing. Most historians think Plato made it up. … But someone received the pass. … An octopus. Eight feet. … De Bruyne counts it as an assist.

**Animation:**
- Searchlights sweep across an empty seabed and find no city.
- An octopus pulls the flat ball over with one arm, juggles it with all eight, and gives a thumbs-up.
- Back on the cliff, De Bruyne's assist counter ticks from **20 → 21**, and an **ASSIST.** stamp lands.

## Facts check
- 20 PL assists in 2019–20, joint record with Thierry Henry (2002–03): premierleague.com/news/1743382
- Atlantis: Plato, *Timaeus* and *Critias* (~360 BC); "beyond the Pillars of Heracles"; sank "in a single day and night"; mainstream view: invented as an allegory.
- Gibraltar → Azores ≈ 1,800 km (sea route Ponta Delgada–Gibraltar ≈ 986 nm ≈ 1,826 km). "Long pass ~60 m" = estimated. 1,800,000 m / 60 m = 30,000.
- Size 5 ball: 410–450 g, circumference 68–70 cm → volume ≈ 5.5 L → floats.
- Average Atlantic depth ≈ 3,600 m; pressure rises by ~1 bar per 10 m → ≈ 360× surface pressure.
- Octopus = 8 arms (the script says "feet" deliberately, as a football joke).
